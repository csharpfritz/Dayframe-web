Privacy Policy

Last Updated: October 24, 2026

Overview

DayFrame is a desktop application that runs on your own computer. It connects to accounts you choose — mail, calendar, contacts, code hosting, social, news and more — and shows them in one place.

This Privacy Policy explains how DayFrame handles information.

There Is No DayFrame Server

DayFrame has no backend. We do not operate a server that your data passes through, we do not have an account system, and we never receive your messages, calendar, contacts, files or credentials. There is nothing for us to hand over, sell or lose, because none of it reaches us.

DayFrame contains no analytics, no telemetry and no crash reporting: nothing about how you use it, or about a crash, is ever sent anywhere. It does keep two kinds of diagnostic file on your computer only, so that a problem can be investigated if you choose to share them:

  • A crash file when DayFrame closes because of an error nothing else handled, holding when it happened, which build was running and the error's technical details. Crash files older than 30 days are deleted at startup.
  • A log of what the app did, one file a day, with the last seven kept.

Both are written to the DayFrame folder under %LOCALAPPDATA%. DayFrame never uploads either one.

What Stays on Your Computer

  • Your cached content — the mail, calendar events, contacts and other items DayFrame has fetched — is stored in a database file, dayframe.vault. Installed from the zip, it is in %LOCALAPPDATA%\DayFrame; installed as a package (including from the Microsoft Store), it is in that package's own local data folder, which Windows deletes when you uninstall the app.
  • That database is encrypted with a 256-bit key generated on your computer the first time DayFrame runs.
  • That key, and your access tokens for every connected account, are held in Windows' own secure storage, not in a file DayFrame writes. So are a mail password or app password for a Yahoo, AOL or other IMAP account (stored only after the mail server has accepted it) and an Asana token you paste in.
  • Your notes are your own files. The Notes module reads and writes a vault folder that you point it at. DayFrame does not copy it anywhere.
  • Your settings — which modules are on, the tile layout, theme, pane zoom, folder choices — are stored in Windows' application settings for DayFrame and are not encrypted. They hold preferences, not message content.

What Leaves Your Computer, and to Whom

Only to the services you connect, and only to the extent needed to show you your own data — or, where a module can change something, to carry out an action you took in DayFrame. Today those actions are:

  • Email sends the messages you write, and moves, archives, deletes and marks messages read or unread when you do.
  • Calendar creates, edits and deletes the events you create, edit and delete.
  • Contacts creates, edits and deletes the contacts you create, edit and delete.
  • Mastodon favorites and bookmarks a post, and undoes either, when you click to.
  • Asana completes and reopens tasks, changes due dates, adds comments and adds tasks.

Every other module only reads. DayFrame talks to:

  • Google (accounts.google.com, oauth2.googleapis.com, gmail.googleapis.com, people.googleapis.com, www.googleapis.com) — sign-in, and your mail, calendar and contacts.
  • Mail providers you connect in Email settings — Yahoo Mail, AOL Mail, iCloud Mail, Posteo, Mailbox.org, WEB.DE, GMX, Telekom, freenet Mail, Vivaldi Webmail and Zoho Mail (the servers for the data center and account type you choose) — and any IMAP and SMTP server you enter yourself, to read and send your mail.
  • Calendar and contacts servers you connect — Posteo, Mailbox.org, Zoho and Yahoo over CalDAV and CardDAV, and any CalDAV or CardDAV server you enter yourself. DayFrame knows the built-in addresses already, so finding them sends nothing anywhere.
  • Your email address's own mail domain, only when you click Find settings while connecting a mail account, or Find server in Calendar settings, to read the server settings your provider publishes (its autoconfig and .well-known addresses, and DNS service records looked up through your computer's normal DNS servers). Only the domain is sent, never the part of your address before the @, and nothing is sent to a third-party settings database.
  • GitHub (github.com, api.github.com) — sign-in, and your issues, pull requests and notifications.
  • Bluesky (bsky.social, bsky.app, cdn.bsky.app) — sign-in, and your timeline.
  • Twitch (id.twitch.tv, api.twitch.tv) — sign-in, and your own channel's live state, title, category, viewer count, follower and subscriber totals and schedule, and which channels you follow are live. DayFrame asks to read your follows (user:read:follows), your follower count (moderator:read:followers) and your subscriber count (channel:read:subscriptions), and nothing else: it can't post, chat or change your channel. Your own profile picture is the one image it loads.
  • The Mastodon server you sign in to — registering DayFrame as an app on that server, sign-in, reading your home timeline, notifications, favorites and bookmarks, and favoriting or bookmarking a post when you click to. DayFrame asks to read your account (read) and to change your favorites and bookmarks (write:favourites, write:bookmarks), and nothing else: it can't post, boost, follow or delete anything. Avatars, attachments, link previews and custom emoji are loaded from the addresses your server gives — normally its own media host.
  • Asana (app.asana.com) — the tasks assigned to you, and the changes you make to them.
  • Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com, geocoding-api.open-meteo.com) — weather for a place you name.
  • Finnhub (finnhub.io) and Yahoo Finance (query1.finance.yahoo.com) — quotes and headlines for tickers you add.
  • Lens by Thought Industries (api.lens.thoughtindustries.com) — answering the questions you type on the Help page. The request carries your question, the Lens API key DayFrame includes (or your own, if you saved one in Help settings) and, for a follow-up, the id of the thread you are continuing. Nothing is sent until you ask a question.
  • ESPN (site.api.espn.com) — scores and headlines for the leagues you follow, once you turn the Sports module on. The request names the league, never the teams you follow.
  • Any RSS or Atom feed you subscribe to — fetching that feed.

Each request goes straight from your computer to that service. If you do not connect an account or add a feed, DayFrame never contacts it. You remain subject to the privacy policies and terms of the services you choose to connect.

Google Sign-In

DayFrame may allow users to connect a Google account. The only account information DayFrame asks Google for is your primary email address, so it can show you which account is connected. It does not request your name or profile image.

Google User Data

If a user connects a Google account, DayFrame requests only the permissions needed for the features that account powers. Each permission, and what DayFrame does with it:

  • Read your Gmail messages (gmail.readonly) — to display your mail inside DayFrame's Email module.
  • Change the state of your Gmail messages (gmail.modify) — to mark messages read or unread, archive them, and move them to Trash when you ask.
  • Send mail on your behalf (gmail.send) — to send the messages you compose in DayFrame. DayFrame never sends mail you did not write and ask it to send.
  • See and change your calendars (calendar) — to show your events and to create, edit or delete the events you create, edit or delete in DayFrame.
  • See and change your contacts (contacts) — to show your contacts and to save the changes you make in DayFrame.
  • See your primary email address (userinfo.email) — so DayFrame can show you which account is connected.

Google data reaches your computer directly from Google. It is not routed through, copied to, or stored on any server operated by DayFrame, because DayFrame operates no such server.

A local copy is kept on your device so the app works offline and does not re-download the same messages repeatedly. That copy is held in the encrypted database described above, and the key for it is stored in Windows' own secure storage, as are the access tokens for every account you connect.

DayFrame does not use Google user data for advertising, does not sell or transfer it, and no human at DayFrame reads it. There is nowhere for anyone at DayFrame to read it from.

Google API Services Limited Use Disclosure

DayFrame's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Remote Content Is Not Trusted

Mail, posts and feed articles are shown in an isolated sandbox with scripts disabled and remote images blocked, so a message cannot run code or quietly load a tracking pixel. If an item contains blocked content, DayFrame says so and lets you allow it for that one item. Allowing images lasts for the rest of the app session and is never written to disk; allowing scripts lasts only while you are looking at that item. Neither choice is ever remembered for a sender, a feed or a domain.

Mastodon posts are the exception. Your Mastodon server already cleans every post, including ones from other servers, before DayFrame receives it, so DayFrame shows that cleaned post as your server formatted it and loads its avatars, attachments and link previews without asking. Those images normally come from your own server's media host; sensitive attachments stay hidden until you click to show them.

Deleting Your Data and Revoking Access

Do these in order: disconnect first, then uninstall or delete. Disconnecting inside DayFrame is what revokes DayFrame's access at the provider, where the provider lets an app do that. Deleting files on your computer removes only what DayFrame already fetched, and leaves the provider's grant live.

  • Delete everything DayFrame holds — Microsoft Store install: disconnect each account (below), then uninstall DayFrame from Windows Settings → Apps. Everything a Store install stores — the encrypted content database, its key, your access tokens and your settings — lives in the package's own data folders, and Windows removes them with the package. Deleting a DayFrame folder in %LOCALAPPDATA% does not delete a Store install's data.
  • Delete everything DayFrame holds — zip install: disconnect each account, close DayFrame, then delete two folders: %LOCALAPPDATA%\DayFrame (the encrypted content database) and %LOCALAPPDATA%\<your user name>\com.csharpfritz.dayframe (Windows' secure storage for the database key and your access tokens, plus your preferences). Deleting only the first folder leaves your access tokens on disk.
  • Your Notes vault is your own folder and neither step touches it.

Disconnecting an account. Each Disconnect clears some things and leaves others:

  • Google — in Settings → Shared Providers, because one Google account serves Email, Calendar and Contacts together. Disconnecting revokes DayFrame's access at Google, deletes the stored credentials, and deletes the cached mail, calendar and contacts. If Google cannot be reached, DayFrame still disconnects locally and says that the access could not be revoked and where to revoke it by hand.
  • GitHub — Disconnect on the GitHub settings page deletes the stored token, every cached issue, pull request and comment, and the tile's content; connecting a different GitHub account does the same. It does not revoke the token at GitHub, which requires a client secret DayFrame does not hold, so the settings page tells you GitHub still lists DayFrame as authorized and links to where you remove it.
  • Bluesky — Disconnect deletes the stored session and the cached timeline and notifications first, then asks your server to end the session. If your server cannot be reached it still disconnects locally and says so; revoke the app password in your Bluesky settings to be sure.
  • Mastodon — Disconnect asks your server to revoke DayFrame's access, forgets the account either way, and if the server did not confirm the revocation tells you to check the server's Authorized apps page.
  • Twitch — Disconnect revokes the token at Twitch, deletes the stored token and the connection, and deletes every cached Twitch item (stream state, viewer samples, schedule and followed channels).
  • Asana — Disconnect deletes the stored personal access token and the connection, and forgets the watched projects and cached tasks. The token itself stays valid at Asana until you delete it there.
  • Yahoo, AOL and other IMAP accounts — Remove on that account in Settings → Email deletes its stored password and the cached mail. The app password stays valid at the provider until you delete it there.

Revoking DayFrame's access at the provider, which you can do whether or not DayFrame is still installed:

  • Google — myaccount.google.com/permissions
  • GitHub — github.com/settings/applications
  • Bluesky — revoke the app password in your Bluesky settings
  • Twitch — twitch.tv/settings/connections, under Other Connections
  • Mastodon — remove DayFrame under Preferences → Account → Authorized apps on your server
  • Yahoo and AOL — delete the app password you created for DayFrame in the account's security settings
  • Asana — delete the personal access token at app.asana.com/0/my-apps. Asana is connected with a token you create and paste in yourself rather than by authorizing an app, so there is no “DayFrame” entry to revoke — and deleting that token also stops anything else you pasted it into.

Revoking access at the provider stops DayFrame fetching anything new; uninstalling, or deleting the local folders, removes what it already fetched.

Data Collection and Sharing

DayFrame does not collect your notes, tasks, email, calendar, contacts, messages, workspace layouts or any other content, and it collects no analytics, behavioral tracking data or usage telemetry. DayFrame does not maintain a centralized database containing user content.

Because none of it reaches DayFrame, DayFrame does not sell, rent, share, or disclose user content to third parties. Information displayed within DayFrame comes from your own computer or from services you have explicitly connected and authorized.

Data Security

DayFrame is designed to keep user information on the user's own device whenever possible.

Users are responsible for securing access to their devices and any third-party accounts connected to DayFrame.

Children's Privacy

DayFrame is not directed to children under the age of 13.
